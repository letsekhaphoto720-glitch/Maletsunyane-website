Where the water turns to smoke

Maletsunyane Falls drops 192 metres in a single leap, into a gorge the Basotho call Lephalo, the Place of Smoke.

Plan your visitSee the photos

One of Africa’s highest single-drop waterfalls

Near the town of Semonkong, the Maletsunyane River spills over a basalt cliff and the spray hangs in the gorge like a cloud. It is a symbol of Lesotho’s rugged beauty and a destination for abseiling, hiking and pony trekking.

Katse Dam

On the Malibamatšo River, Katse is Africa’s second-largest double-curvature arch dam, after the Tekezé Dam in Ethiopia. It sits below the meeting of the Bokong River, which forms the western arm of the reservoir.

The project was first identified by South African engineer Ninham Shand in 1953 as a way to supply water to the Witwatersrand. The World Bank then helped broker a treaty between South Africa and Lesotho.
